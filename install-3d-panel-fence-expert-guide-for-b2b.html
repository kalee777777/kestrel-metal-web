1. Understanding 3D Panel Fence and Its Variants

What Is a 3D Panel Fence?

A 3D panel fence is a welded wire mesh panel in which horizontal and vertical wires are welded at every intersection, with the horizontal wires pressed into a V-shaped bend. This bending creates the characteristic "3D" profile that gives the panel its rigidity and visual depth. The panels are typically mounted on steel posts using metal clamps and security bolts, forming a strong, tamper-resistant perimeter barrier.

The system is popular in commercial, industrial, and residential applications because it balances three critical requirements: strength, visibility, and aesthetic appeal. Unlike chain-link fencing, a 3D panel fence resists cutting and climbing far more effectively, while maintaining an open, clean appearance that suits modern architecture and urban environments.

Key specifications to consider when specifying or purchasing 3D panels include:

  • Wire diameter: Commonly 4mm, 5mm, or 6mm for vertical and horizontal wires, depending on security level.
  • Mesh size: Standard openings range from 50x200mm to 76.2x12.7mm for high-security variants.
  • Panel height: Typically 1.0m to 2.4m, with custom heights available for specific projects.
  • Coating options: Hot-dip galvanized, electro-galvanized, or powder-coated in a range of RAL colors.

Clear View Fence: The Transparent Security Solution

The clear view fence, also known as the 358 fence, is a high-security variant of the welded mesh panel system. Its name comes from its mesh configuration: 3-inch by 0.5-inch by 8-gauge wire, which translates to a mesh opening of approximately 76.2x12.7mm. This small aperture makes the fence extremely difficult to climb and almost impossible to cut with standard hand tools.

Clear view fence is widely specified for airports, prisons, data centers, and other high-security sites where visibility and anti-climb performance are paramount. The tight mesh allows for clear sightlines while providing a formidable physical barrier. Because of the smaller openings and typically thicker wires, clear view fence panels are heavier and require closer post spacing to withstand wind loads.

Double Wire Fence: Strength in Twin Horizontals

The double wire fence uses two horizontal wires welded to each vertical wire, creating an exceptionally rigid panel. A common configuration is 8mm horizontal wires paired with 6mm vertical wires in a 50x200mm mesh. This twin-horizontal design delivers superior resistance to impact and deformation, making it a favorite for sports facilities, industrial perimeters, and public infrastructure.

Other important variants in this product family include:

  • BRC fence: BRC stands for British Reinforced Concrete, and this welded mesh fence is often used with concrete posts or steel posts. It shares the 50x200mm mesh profile and is a cost-effective choice for boundary demarcation and light security.
  • Wire partition panel: A modular welded mesh panel used for internal partitioning in warehouses, factories, and workshops. It creates secure storage cages and machine guards without the visual weight of solid walls.
  • Concrete BRC roll: A welded wire mesh roll used as reinforcement in concrete slabs, walls, and foundations, providing tensile strength and crack control.

2. Pre-Installation Planning and Site Preparation

Assessing Site Conditions and Permits

Before you install 3D panel fence on any site, proper planning will save time, money, and rework. Start by checking local regulations and obtaining any required permits. Many jurisdictions have height restrictions, setback requirements, and planning approvals for perimeter fencing, especially in commercial and industrial zones.

Next, assess soil conditions. Sandy or loose soils may require deeper post holes or wider concrete footings, while rocky ground may call for mechanical augers or rock-breaking equipment. If the site is sloped, you will need to decide between stepped installation (panels follow the gradient in steps) or racked installation (panels follow the slope continuously). Stepped installation is more common for 3D panel fence systems because the panels are rigid and cannot be easily racked.

Tip: Always check for underground utilities before digging. Contact your local utility locating service and review site drawings for water, gas, electrical, and fiber optic lines. Striking a utility line can cause injury, project delays, and significant financial liability.

Gathering Tools and Materials

A well-organized tool and material list keeps the installation on schedule. For a typical 3D panel fence project, you will need:

  • Post hole digger or mechanical auger
  • Spirit level (600mm and 1.2m)
  • String line and marking paint
  • Measuring tape and stakes
  • Wrench set and socket set
  • Concrete mixer or ready-mix concrete
  • Bolt cutters and crimping tools
  • Safety equipment (gloves, goggles, high-visibility vests)

Materials include the 3D panels, steel posts, panel clamps, security bolts, concrete, and gate hardware. For clear view fence installations, ensure you have the correct clamps for the thicker wire diameters. For double wire fence projects, verify that the clamps accommodate the twin horizontal wires.

Setting Out the Fence Line

Setting out the fence line accurately is critical. Use string and stakes to mark the perimeter, then mark post positions at the recommended spacing. For standard 3D panel fence, post spacing is usually 2.5m to 3m. For clear view fence, posts may need to be closer, typically 2.4m or less, due to the higher wind load and panel weight.

Walk the line and check for obstructions such as trees, drainage channels, and existing structures. Adjust the layout as needed before digging. A few minutes spent verifying the line will prevent costly adjustments later. If you are integrating a wire partition panel system indoors, set out the frame positions with the same care, ensuring that modular panels align with building columns and walls.

3. Step-by-Step Installation of 3D Panel Fence

Digging Post Holes and Setting Posts

Once the fence line is set, dig post holes to the required depth and diameter. For most 3D panel fence installations, holes should be 600mm to 800mm deep, depending on soil conditions and wind load requirements. The hole diameter should be at least three times the post diameter to allow adequate concrete cover.

  1. Dig each hole to the marked depth and diameter.
  2. Place the post in the hole and check it for plumb using a spirit level.
  3. Brace the post temporarily with stakes and string to hold it vertical.
  4. Pour concrete around the post, tamping it down to remove air pockets.
  5. Recheck plumb and alignment before the concrete begins to set.
  6. Allow the concrete to cure for at least 24 hours, preferably 48 hours in cold weather.

For BRC fence installations using concrete posts, ensure the posts are set on a level concrete foundation and that the panel mounting holes align correctly. For steel posts, follow the same concreting procedure as standard 3D panel fence.

Attaching Panels to Posts

After the concrete has cured, attach the panels to the posts using clamps and bolts. Start from one end and work along the fence line, keeping the top of each panel level with the previous one.

  • Position the panel against the post and align the mesh with the clamp holes.
  • Attach the clamp and insert the security bolt, tightening it finger-tight first.
  • Check alignment and tension before fully tightening.
  • For double wire fence, ensure the double horizontal wires are positioned correctly and that clamps grip both wires evenly.
  • For clear view fence, take extra care with alignment because the small mesh makes any misalignment more visible.

Work methodically along the line, checking that panels are level and that the fence follows the intended line. If the ground slopes, step the panels so that each panel is level and the step occurs at the post.

Installing Gates and Accessories

Gates should be installed after the main fence line is complete. Position gate posts first, ensuring they are set deeper and with more concrete than standard posts to handle the additional load. Hang the gate and check that it swings freely without binding.

Install bracing where required, especially at corners, gate posts, and terminal posts. If you are integrating a wire partition panel system with existing structures, install the frame and modular panels according to the manufacturer's layout, ensuring that all connections are secure and that the panels are level.

Tip: Do not overtighten bolts. Over-tightening can deform the wire mesh and weaken the coating, leading to premature corrosion. Tighten until the clamp is snug and the panel is secure, then stop.

4. Special Considerations for Different Fence Types

Installing Clear View Fence for High-Security Areas

Clear view fence requires precise alignment because its small mesh openings make any deviation obvious. The panels are typically heavier than standard 3D panels, so two installers are recommended for safe handling. Use thicker posts and closer spacing to handle wind loads, and consider adding razor wire or spike toppings for high-security applications.

Specifications for clear view fence commonly include:

  • Mesh: 76.2x12.7mm
  • Wire diameter: 4mm
  • Panel height: 1.8m to 3.0m
  • Coating: Hot-dip galvanized or powder-coated

Working with Concrete BRC Roll in Reinforced Applications

The concrete BRC roll is not a standalone fence product. It is a welded wire mesh roll used as reinforcement in concrete structures such as slabs, walls, and foundations. It provides tensile strength to concrete and helps prevent cracking. When used in concrete fencing systems, the BRC roll is embedded within the concrete wall or footing to add structural integrity.

Typical specifications for concrete BRC roll include wire diameters of 6mm to 12mm, with mesh sizes varying according to the structural requirement. Always follow engineering drawings and local building codes when specifying reinforcement mesh.

Integrating Wire Partition Panels in Industrial Settings

The wire partition panel is a modular welded mesh panel used for internal partitioning. It is ideal for creating secure storage cages, tool rooms, and machine guards within warehouses and factories. Installation involves mounting panels to posts and frames, often with a top rail and base plate for stability.

Common dimensions for wire partition panels are 1.2m to 2.4m in height and 1m to 3m in width, with mesh openings ranging from 50x50mm to 100x100mm. They are modular and can be customized to fit specific spaces. When integrating wire partition panels with an existing building, ensure that the frame is anchored securely to the floor and walls.

These products often complement each other in a comprehensive security solution. A perimeter of 3D panel fence or double wire fence can be paired with clear view fence in high-security zones and wire partition panels indoors, all supplied by a single manufacturer such as Kestrel Metal.

5. Quality Assurance and Maintenance After Installation

Inspecting the Installation for Compliance

After installation, conduct a thorough inspection before handing the project over. Check that all posts are vertical, panels are secure, and gates operate smoothly. Verify that the fence line follows the approved layout and that all bolts are tightened to specification.

  • Check post plumb with a spirit level at multiple points.
  • Verify panel alignment and tension along the entire run.
  • Test gate operation, including latches and hinges.
  • Confirm that all clamps and bolts are present and secure.
  • Inspect coating for scratches or damage and touch up as needed.

Maintenance Tips for Longevity

Regular maintenance extends the life of your 3D panel fence and preserves its appearance. Inspect the fence at least twice a year for rust, loose bolts, and damage. Tighten bolts as needed and touch up any scratches with anti-rust paint. Clean with water and mild detergent to remove dirt and debris.

With galvanized or powder-coated finishes, maintenance is minimal. However, in coastal or industrial environments, more frequent inspections are recommended due to exposure to salt and pollutants.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced installers can make mistakes that compromise the fence's performance. Avoid these common pitfalls:

  • Insufficient concrete curing: Setting panels before concrete has cured can cause posts to shift.
  • Misaligned posts: Small errors at the start compound along the line.
  • Overtightening bolts: This can damage the wire coating and weaken the panel.
  • Ignoring wind load: Especially important for clear view fence and double wire fence installations.
  • Using low-quality materials: Always source from reputable manufacturers to ensure durability and safety.

6. Why Choose 3D Panel Fence for Your Project?

Cost-Effectiveness and Durability

The 3D panel fence offers an excellent strength-to-weight ratio, which reduces material and transportation costs compared to heavier alternatives. With proper coating, it can last 20 years or more with minimal maintenance. For B2B buyers, this translates to lower total cost of ownership over the life of the asset.

Aesthetic and Security Benefits

Beyond security, 3D panel fencing delivers a clean, modern appearance that enhances property value. The clear view fence provides unobstructed visibility for surveillance, while the double wire fence offers superior rigidity for high-traffic areas. Both options can be powder-coated in custom colors to match branding or architectural requirements.

Environmental and Sustainability Considerations

Steel is one of the most recycled materials in the world. 3D panel fence systems are fully recyclable at end of life, and their installation has minimal site impact compared to concrete walls or masonry fencing. For projects targeting green building certifications, welded mesh fencing is a sustainable choice.
