1. What Is a 358 Fence? Understanding High Security Mesh Fencing

Definition and Origin of the 358 Fence

A 358 fence is a welded mesh fencing system engineered specifically for high-security perimeters. The name is a direct reference to its geometry: a 3-inch × 0.5-inch mesh aperture (76.2mm × 12.7mm) formed from 8-gauge wire (approximately 4mm diameter). Hence "3-5-8."

The format originated in the United Kingdom corrections and transport sectors, where traditional chain link proved too easy to cut and climb. It has since become the global benchmark for detention, aviation, and critical infrastructure fencing — often specified under names such as "358 security mesh," "prison mesh," or "anti-climb mesh."

How 358 Mesh Fencing Works

The security of a 358 fence comes from two design principles working together:

  • Anti-climb geometry: The 12.7mm horizontal aperture is too narrow for fingers or toes to gain purchase. There are no handholds, so the fence cannot be scaled without tools.
  • Anti-cut construction: Each intersection of the welded mesh is a fused joint. Standard bolt cutters, hacksaws, and hand tools cannot sever the 4mm wires within a practical timeframe, and the tight mesh spacing prevents lever-type cutting tools from being inserted.

The result is a fence that delays intrusion long enough for detection and response — the core objective of any layered security strategy.

Key Applications Across Industries

358 fencing is deployed wherever the consequence of breach is high:

  • Airports: Runway and apron perimeter fencing with clear sightlines for CCTV.
  • Correctional facilities: Prison perimeters, exercise yards, and internal segregation walls.
  • Military and government: Bases, ammunition stores, and embassies.
  • Data centers: Server campus perimeters requiring both physical and visual security.
  • Warehouses and logistics: High-value inventory yards and distribution hubs.
  • Utilities: Substations, water treatment plants, and energy infrastructure.

Post Systems, Fixings, and Coating Choices

Panel performance depends on the post system. Common configurations include:

  • Square posts (e.g., 60×60mm or 80×80mm): Cost-effective and easy to align; popular for commercial perimeters.
  • Round posts: Often used with clamp bars for a clean, tamper-resistant finish.
  • I-beam posts: The preferred choice for airports, prisons, and military sites due to superior rigidity and wind-load resistance.

Fixings typically consist of steel clamp bars, M8 security bolts, and shear nuts that cannot be undone with standard tools. Coating options include:

  • Hot-dip galvanized: Zinc coating (typically 40–60µm or higher) for long-term corrosion protection.
  • PVC-coated: Added UV-stable polymer layer in green, black, or custom RAL colors.
  • Powder-coated: Aesthetic finish for visible commercial installations.

3. Choose Mesh 358 Mesh Airport Perimeter Fencing: Key Considerations

Why Airports Require 358 Fencing

When security teams choose mesh 358 mesh airport perimeter fencing, they are responding to a unique threat profile. Airport perimeters are long, often remote, and must deter intrusion without blocking radar, sightlines, or wildlife management operations.

358 mesh delivers three airport-critical benefits:

  1. Clear visibility: The thin 4mm wire and open aperture allow unobstructed CCTV coverage and patrol sightlines.
  2. Anti-climb, anti-cut performance: Meets ICAO and national aviation security guidance for perimeter hardening.
  3. Integration-ready: Panels accept fence-mounted detection sensors, vibration cables, and CCTV poles without modification.

Installation Best Practices for Airports

Airport installations demand precision. Key practices include:

  • Grounding and earthing: Metal fences near navigational aids must be bonded and earthed to prevent static and lightning issues.
  • Tensioning and alignment: Panels should be set with consistent tension so the mesh cannot be pried apart at posts.
  • Gate compatibility: Vehicle gates, pedestrian turnstiles, and emergency egress points must match the fence's security rating.
  • Buried mesh or concrete plinths: Prevents digging under the fence line.

Integration with Surveillance and Access Control

A 358 fence is a delay layer, not a standalone solution. Best practice is to combine it with:

  • Perimeter CCTV with analytics (loitering, crossing detection)
  • Fiber-optic or vibration fence sensors
  • Microwave or infrared perimeter intrusion detection
  • ANPR and biometric access control at gates

4. Anti Climb Fence: How 358 Fencing Prevents Intrusion

Design Features That Deter Climbing

An anti climb fence works by removing the physical affordances climbers rely on. In a 358 fence:

  • The 12.7mm aperture is narrower than a human finger, so no grip points exist.
  • The 4mm wire is too thin to stand on and too strong to deform under body weight.
  • The flush panel-to-post connection eliminates climbing steps at joints.

Combined with a minimum height of 2.4m — and commonly 3m or more — the fence presents no viable climbing route.

5. Australia Temporary Fence and Temporary Fence Solutions

When to Use Temporary Fencing

Not every high-security requirement is permanent. A temporary fence is the right choice for:

  • Construction and civil works sites
  • Public events, festivals, and sporting venues
  • Emergency response and disaster zones
  • Short-term high-value storage yards

Australia Temporary Fence: Standards and Compliance

In Australia, temporary fencing must comply with AS 4687 — the Australian Standard for temporary fencing and hoardings. Key requirements include panel dimensions, foot design, and wind-load resistance. Buyers specifying an Australia temporary fence should confirm:

  • Compliance with AS 4687-1 and AS 4687-2
  • Galvanized finish for coastal and outdoor exposure
  • Anti-lift brackets and couplers to prevent panel removal
  • Concrete or blow-molded bases rated for local wind conditions

Rental vs. Purchase for Short-Term Projects

The rental-versus-purchase decision depends on project duration and frequency:

  • Rental: Best for projects under 6 months, one-off events, or when storage and transport are impractical.
  • Purchase: More economical for contractors with recurring projects, long timelines, or ongoing site security needs.

7. Installation, Maintenance, and Cost Guide for 358 Fence

Step-by-Step Installation Overview

  1. Site survey: Confirm boundary line, ground conditions, wind loading, and utility locations.
  2. Post setting: Excavate and concrete posts at correct spacing and depth (typically 600–900mm below grade).
  3. Panel attachment: Lift panels into position and secure with clamp bars and security bolts.
  4. Tensioning and alignment: Check verticality, line, and mesh tension before final tightening.
  5. Toppings and gates: Install razor wire, barbed tape, or spike toppings and hang gates last.

Maintenance Tips for Longevity

  • Inspect panels and posts annually for corrosion, impact damage, or loose fixings.
  • Wash coastal installations to remove salt deposits.
  • Touch up any coating damage with zinc-rich paint or matching PVC repair compound.
  • Re-tension clamp bars after the first 12 months, then every 2–3 years.
