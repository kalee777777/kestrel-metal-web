New Alloy Coating Exceeds 3,000-Hour Salt Spray Benchmark

July 28, 2026 5 min read
Back to Insight

A New Benchmark in Corrosion Protection

After three years of formulation development and accelerated weathering trials, our R&D center has confirmed that Kestrel Metal's latest zinc-aluminum alloy coating has passed 3,000+ hours of neutral salt spray testing with no red rust — the most demanding corrosion result ever recorded in our laboratory. The milestone doubles the performance of conventional hot-dip galvanizing on comparable wire diameters and marks the culmination of a research program that combined material science, electrochemistry, and hundreds of iterative bath trials.

Why 3,000 Hours Matters

Salt spray testing to ASTM B117 is the industry's standard method for comparing corrosion protection. One hour in the chamber approximates weeks of real-world atmospheric exposure, so every additional thousand hours signals a meaningful extension of service life — especially for infrastructure along coastlines, highways treated with de-icing salt, and industrial sites with aggressive chemical atmospheres. The table below shows how the new formulation compares with the coating systems our customers specify most often.

Coating SystemTime to Red Rust (ASTM B117)Typical Environment
Regular pre-galvanized wire300–500 hoursIndoor, dry outdoor
Hot-dip galvanized (post-woven)1,000–1,500 hoursRural, urban outdoor
Galfan (Zn–5% Al)2,000–2,500 hoursCoastal, industrial
New Kestrel Zn–Al alloy3,000+ hoursMarine, severe industrial

Inside the Formulation

The breakthrough comes from a rebalanced zinc-aluminum matrix enriched with selective micro-alloying elements. Aluminum content stabilizes the coating's barrier behavior, while the modified composition refines the intermetallic layer at the steel interface — the region where conventional coatings typically fail first. The result is a coating that adheres tightly through weaving, welding, and bending, with self-healing behavior that seals minor scratches and cut edges before corrosion can take hold.

Validated Beyond the Chamber

Laboratory results only matter if they hold up in the field. Alongside chamber testing, the coating has completed 18 months of exposure trials at marine and industrial pilot sites, attached to live perimeter and gabion installations. Inspections at 6, 12, and 18 months confirmed uniform appearance, no flaking, and no under-film creep at cross-scribed test zones. Welded joints — historically the weakest point of any coated mesh — retained full coating integrity after cyclic load testing.

What This Means for Your Projects

The new alloy coating is now available as an option across our welded wire mesh panels, woven rolls, and gabion product families. For specifiers, it means longer maintenance intervals and lower lifecycle cost in aggressive environments. For contractors, it means a single proven coating system that satisfies the most stringent anti-corrosion clauses without moving to premium stainless steel grades.
