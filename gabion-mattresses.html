Product Information

Gabion mattresses, also known as reno mattresses, are thin, flat wire mesh units designed specifically for erosion protection on riverbeds, channel bottoms, and sloped surfaces. Their low-profile geometry allows them to conform to irregular terrain while providing a stable, erosion-resistant foundation.

Manufactured from welded steel wire with galvanized or PVC coated finishes, gabion mattresses offer excellent flexibility and durability in hydraulic environments. The permeable structure allows water to flow through while the stone fill provides mass and stability against scouring forces.

Where Reno Mattresses Are Used

Gabion mattresses are specified wherever water or gravity attacks soil surfaces and a flexible, permeable armour layer is required. Typical applications include:

  • Riverbank and shoreline protection — absorbing flow energy without trapping sediment behind a rigid wall
  • Channel and drainage lining — continuous erosion-resistant coverage on beds and side slopes
  • Scour protection — at bridge piers, culvert outfalls and weir foundations where turbulence undermines structures
  • Slope stabilisation — holding fill and topsoil on embankments while allowing vegetation to establish through the stone fill

Installation in Four Steps

  1. Prepare the foundation — grade the slope or bed, remove debris, and place a geotextile filter layer so fine soil cannot wash through the stone fill
  2. Assemble and position — fold out the mattress, tension the diaphragms, and wire adjacent units together so the lining acts as one continuous mat
  3. Fill with stone — use well-graded, angular stone within the specified range (typically 70–150 mm for standard units), packed by hand at exposed faces for a tight, even surface
  4. Close and lace — fasten lids with lacing wire at every mesh opening, then brush soil and seed over vegetated slopes if required

Frequently Asked Questions

Mattress or gabion box — which one does my project need?

Choose mattresses for surface protection: lining, revetment and scour work where the unit lies flat and thin. Choose boxes for retaining walls and structures that build height and carry structural load. Many hydraulic projects combine both — boxes at the toe, mattresses across the bed and slope.

What stone should be used for filling?

Angular, durable stone sized so it cannot pass through the mesh opening — for standard 60×80 mm openings this means roughly 70–150 mm stone, matching the specification table above. Stone durability matters more than origin: it must resist freezing, wetting and abrasion over the structure’s design life.

How do welded mattresses differ from woven (double-twist)?

Welded mattresses hold their rectangular shape precisely, giving a tidier face and faster installation on engineered slopes; woven units flex more when the foundation settles irregularly. For machine-filled, high-production channel works the welded format is usually faster; for settlement-prone riverbeds, discuss the woven alternative with our engineers.
