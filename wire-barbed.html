Product Overview

Reliable Barbed Wire for Perimeter Security

Barbed wire is a classic and time-tested fencing solution consisting of twisted wire strands with sharp barbs spaced at regular intervals. It provides an effective physical and psychological deterrent for a wide range of applications, from agricultural fencing to high-security perimeter protection.

Manufactured from high-quality carbon steel, our barbed wire is available in both galvanized and PVC-coated finishes to ensure maximum durability and corrosion resistance in all weather conditions. Advanced automated machinery guarantees consistent barb spacing, uniform twist, and reliable tensile strength across every roll.

  • Single twist & double twist configurations
  • Barb spacing 75–150mm (3"–6")
  • Wire gauge 12–18 ga (2.5–1.2mm)
  • Galvanized or PVC coated finish
  • Roll lengths 200–500m
  • Tensile strength 350–900 MPa

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about barbed wire selection, configuration, and installation.

What gauge and barb spacing should I choose?
Standard barbed wire uses a 12–13 gauge core wire with a lighter barb gauge and a barb spacing of 3" to 6". Closer spacing (3"–4") provides a denser, more aggressive barrier, ideal for high-security topping. Wider spacing (5"–6") balances material economy for long agricultural runs. Choose 12ga for strength or thinner gauges for lighter duty.
Galvanized or PVC coated — which finish is better?
Hot-dip galvanized barbed wire offers excellent all-round corrosion resistance and is the most economical choice for most projects, lasting decades in rural and urban settings. PVC-coated wire adds a smooth protective layer that suits coastal, humid, or chemically aggressive environments and is available in green or black. For aggressive coastal conditions, prefer PVC or heavy galvanizing.
How many strands of barbed wire do I need?
For livestock, 3 to 5 strands are typical depending on animal size and fence height — cattle calves may use 4–5 tightly spaced strands, while adult cattle do well with 3–4. For security-topping on mesh fences, a single to three strands run along the top or angled outriggers. Add strands near the bottom for smaller animals and near the top for climbing deterrence.
Single twist or double twist — what's the difference?
Single twist barbed wire consists of two strands twisted together and is the traditional, economical standard. Double twist uses a more robust construction that resists unwinding and sagging under tension, making it stronger and more durable for long spans and heavier duty. Choose double twist where maximum strength and longevity matter.
Is barbed wire suitable for livestock?
Yes, barbed wire is widely used for cattle, sheep, and ranch fencing. The barbs act as a strong physical deterrent that encourages animals to respect the boundary. Because barbs can injure closely confined animals, many livestock producers prefer non-barbed field fence or high-tensile wire for horses and confined pens, reserving barbed wire for perimeter and cattle fencing.
How much barbed wire do I need for my fence?
Estimate fence length and multiply by the number of strands. Add 5–10% for sag, knots, start/end wrapping, and corners. For a 500m fence with 4 strands, you'll need roughly 2,100m of wire. Rolls are typically 200–500m, so plan purchases to minimize joins. Contact us with your layout for an exact quantity recommendation.
How long does galvanized barbed wire last?
Hot-dip galvanized barbed wire typically lasts 15 to 30 years or more in most environments, depending on coating weight, climate, and installation quality. PVC-coated wire offers similar longevity where the coating stays intact. High-humidity or coastal installations benefit from heavier galvanizing to extend service life.
Can barbed wire be combined with chain link or welded fences?
Yes. Barbed wire is commonly installed along the top of chain link or welded mesh fences, either on the post top or on angled outrigger arms, to boost security and deter climbing. It can also be run along the bottom kick-rail to deter burrowing animals. This combination offers visual transparency with an effective barrier.
