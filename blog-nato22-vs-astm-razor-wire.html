NATO-22 vs ASTM Razor Wire: Standards, Specifications & Comparison Guide

August 21, 2026 10 min read
Back to Blog & News

NATO-22 (BTO-22 blade type, also known as BT-22) is a military-grade razor wire standard specifying 21–22 mm blade height, 2.5 mm blade width, 2.5 mm core wire diameter, 1770 MPa core wire tensile strength, hot-dip galvanized zinc coating of minimum 275 g/m², and available coil diameters from 300 mm to 1250 mm, commonly deployed on concertina coils measuring 450–980 mm expanded diameter. ASTM F2781, the American Society for Testing and Materials standard for razor wire and barbed tape, defines equivalent blade geometry, tensile strength thresholds, and corrosion resistance test requirements including salt spray duration. In practice, NATO-22 and ASTM F2781 cover largely overlapping material and dimensional specifications, but differ in testing protocol emphasis and procurement classification language. What Each Standard Means

NATO-22 derives from the MIL-AA-55522A military specification, the foundational standard originally developed by the United States Department of Defense and subsequently adopted across NATO member nations for barbed tape concertina (BTC) procurement. The “22” designation refers specifically to the 21–22 mm blade height, which is the defining dimensional parameter that classifies the wire as a medium-to-high security deterrent blade type. NATO-22 governs not only blade geometry but also core wire mechanical properties (minimum tensile strength of 1770 MPa for high-carbon spring steel), galvanization requirements (minimum zinc mass of 275 g/m² per area, equivalent to G275 class), and coil deployment characteristics including free expansion diameter, clip spacing, and recovery behavior after deformation. Compliance with NATO-22 is a mandatory procurement requirement for military applications in most NATO-aligned countries and is recognized globally as the benchmark for high-security perimeter razor wire.

ASTM F2781 (Standard Specification for Razor Wire) is the equivalent civilian and commercial market standard published by the American Society for Testing and Materials. While the dimensional and material specifications in ASTM F2781 closely mirror NATO-22 for blade types in the BTO-22 class, the ASTM standard places additional emphasis on standardized test methods for corrosion resistance (referencing ASTM B117 salt spray testing), blade sharpness retention under accelerated weathering, and tensile strength verification procedures. ASTM F2781 also covers the full spectrum of razor wire blade types—from short-blade CBT-25 through long-blade CBT-65—providing a unified classification system for commercial, industrial, and critical infrastructure applications that do not require military procurement documentation. For international buyers, ASTM F2781 compliance is the more commonly specified requirement in private security, border control, and infrastructure projects outside of direct military procurement channels.

Blade Geometry Explained

The blade profile is the single most critical differentiator between razor wire standards and the primary factor that determines both deterrent capability and deployment configuration. BTO-22 (NATO-22) blades feature a symmetrical cross-cut geometry with a blade height of 21–22 mm, a blade width of 2.5 mm, and a pressed center ridge that provides structural rigidity while creating sharp cutting edges on both sides of the blade. The blade spacing along the steel strip is typically 100–110 mm, which means each linear meter of razor wire tape carries approximately 9–10 individual blade points. When wrapped around a 2.5 mm high-carbon spring steel core wire and formed into concertina coils, the BTO-22 blade profile delivers a balance of cutting resistance and coil packing density that is ideal for medium-to-high security perimeter applications.

CBT-65 blades are significantly larger at 65 mm height, using a fish-hook or crescent-shaped profile that creates a self-locking effect when the blade catches on clothing, equipment, or skin. The fish-hook geometry means that once a blade engages, withdrawal force actually tightens the grip, making CBT-65 the most aggressive deterrent profile in the razor wire product family. CBT-65 is typically deployed on coils with larger diameters (730–1250 mm) to accommodate the increased blade profile size, and the expanded deployment width is substantially greater than BTO-22 configurations. CBT-25, by contrast, uses a shorter 25 mm blade with narrower overall dimensions, making it the most compact and cost-effective option for commercial perimeters where deterrence is the primary objective and aggressive cutting capability is not required.

When selecting between NATO-22/BTO-22, CBT-65, and CBT-25 blade types, always align the choice with a formal threat assessment. BTO-22 is the military standard baseline for medium-high security; CBT-65 is reserved for maximum-deterrence scenarios where breach consequences are critical; CBT-25 is appropriate for commercial and industrial sites where visible deterrence is the primary goal.

Zinc Coating & Corrosion Resistance

Galvanization is the primary corrosion protection mechanism for razor wire deployed in outdoor environments, and the zinc coating mass directly determines the service life of the product in aggressive atmospheric conditions. Both NATO-22 and ASTM F2781 specify a minimum hot-dip galvanized zinc coating of 275 g/m² (equivalent to G275 per EN 10244), which provides a robust sacrificial zinc layer over the high-carbon steel core wire and blade strip. At this coating weight, the zinc layer provides cathodic protection to the underlying steel even when the surface is scratched or mechanically damaged during installation, because the zinc anode preferentially corrodes in the galvanic couple, shielding the steel cathode from oxidation.

Salt spray testing per ASTM B117 provides the standardized accelerated corrosion benchmark used to compare coating durability across manufacturers and product lines. Kestrel Metal’s galvanized razor wire consistently exceeds 200 hours of continuous salt spray exposure (5% NaCl solution at 35°C) without visible red rust on the core wire or blade surface, meeting the ASTM F2781 requirement for G275-class galvanized products. For applications in coastal, marine, or high-humidity environments where salt spray exposure is prolonged, Kestrel Metal offers PVC-coated razor wire that extends salt spray resistance to over 500 hours before substrate corrosion initiates. The PVC coating provides an additional barrier layer on top of the galvanization, creating a dual-protection system where the PVC prevents direct chloride contact with the zinc surface and the zinc provides cathodic backup protection if the PVC layer is mechanically breached.

For specification purposes, the practical implication of salt spray test data is that galvanized razor wire (200+ hours ASTM B117) is suitable for most inland and moderate coastal installations with a projected service life of 15–20 years, while PVC-coated razor wire (500+ hours ASTM B117) is recommended for direct coastal exposure, marine environments, and chemical processing facility perimeters where chloride or sulfur dioxide concentrations exceed typical atmospheric levels. Kestrel Metal provides salt spray test certificates with every shipment upon request, documenting the specific test duration, specimen location on the coil, and pass/fail result against the applicable standard threshold.

Deployment Applications

Military base perimeter security is the primary deployment scenario for NATO-22/BTO-22 razor wire, where the standard is most commonly specified in conjunction with chain-link or welded mesh fencing to create layered defense zones. Standard military deployment typically involves a minimum of three concertina coil rows arranged in a linear, over-under configuration along the fence line, with additional flat-wrap razor wire attached to the fence fabric using galvanized wire ties at 300 mm intervals. The three-row configuration creates a barrier zone approximately 1.2–1.5 meters wide that delays and deters unauthorized access while allowing clear sightlines for surveillance cameras and sentry positions.

Border perimeter applications use NATO-22 and CBT-65 razor wire in single-row or double-row concertina configurations, often deployed in remote locations where maintenance access is limited and long-term corrosion resistance is paramount. CBT-65 fish-hook blade profiles are increasingly specified for border applications because the self-locking blade geometry provides enhanced deterrent capability without requiring the multi-row configurations that BTO-22 typically demands. Prison and correctional facility fencing combines BTO-22 concertina topping with flat-wrap razor wire attached directly to the fence fabric, with specifications often requiring 2–3 rows of concertina coils on top of the fence plus continuous flat-wrap from the top rail to a height of 2.0 meters. Airport perimeter security uses a combination of razor wire types depending on the zone classification: BTO-22 for general perimeter zones and CBT-65 for restricted areas adjacent to runways and aircraft parking areas where the highest level of breach deterrence is required.

Installation Best Practices

Correct post spacing is the foundation of a secure razor wire installation. For concertina coil deployments, steel Y-posts or T-posts should be installed at 2.5–3.0 meter intervals along the fence line, with a minimum embedment depth of 600 mm in soil or 400 mm into concrete footings for hard-surface installations. Posts must be plumb and aligned within ±15 mm of true vertical across the full installation run, because misaligned posts create uneven tension on the concertina coils, leading to sagging sections that reduce both the visual deterrent effect and the physical barrier width. For flat-wrap razor wire installed directly on chain-link or welded mesh fence fabric, the maximum recommended post spacing is 2.0 meters to prevent the fence fabric from bowing under the dead weight of the razor wire attachment.

Clip intervals for securing concertina coils to the fence structure should be maintained at 300–500 mm, using galvanized steel clips, U-bolts, or tie wire (minimum 2.0 mm galvanized wire) at each attachment point. The clip interval directly affects the coil’s ability to maintain its expanded diameter under wind load and attempted breach conditions: clips spaced wider than 500 mm allow the coil to compress under lateral force, reducing the effective barrier width and creating exploitable gaps. For military-specification installations (NATO-22), clip intervals of 300 mm are standard, with double-clip attachment at each post location (one clip at the top coil circumference and one at the bottom) to prevent rotation of the coil around the post axis. All clip materials should be hot-dip galvanized to a minimum of 275 g/m² zinc coating to match the corrosion resistance of the razor wire itself, preventing premature clip failure that would compromise the installation integrity before the razor wire reaches its end of service life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between NATO-22 and ASTM F2781 razor wire?

A: NATO-22 (derived from MIL-AA-55522A) and ASTM F2781 cover largely overlapping specifications for BTO-22 class razor wire, including 21–22 mm blade height, 2.5 mm wire diameter, 1770 MPa tensile strength, and minimum 275 g/m² zinc coating. The primary difference is that NATO-22 is a military procurement standard with specific logistics and documentation requirements, while ASTM F2781 is a commercial testing standard with emphasis on standardized salt spray testing (ASTM B117) and blade sharpness retention protocols.

Q: What coil diameters are available for BTO-22 concertina razor wire?

A: BTO-22 concertina razor wire is manufactured in coil diameters ranging from 300 mm (single coil flat-wrap applications) to 980 mm (standard military concertina), with the most common sizes being 450 mm, 600 mm, 730 mm, and 980 mm expanded diameter. Kestrel Metal supplies all standard coil sizes and can produce custom diameters to specification for special deployment requirements.

Q: How long does galvanized razor wire last in coastal environments?

A: Standard hot-dip galvanized razor wire (minimum 275 g/m² zinc coating per ASTM F2781 / NATO-22) provides 15–20 years of service life in inland and moderate coastal environments, validated by 200+ hours of salt spray testing per ASTM B117. For direct coastal or marine environments with high salt concentration, PVC-coated razor wire extends service life to 25+ years with 500+ hours of salt spray resistance. Kestrel Metal recommends PVC coating for any installation within 5 km of the coastline.

Q: What post spacing and clip intervals are recommended for concertina razor wire installation?

A: Concertina coils should be supported on steel posts spaced at 2.5–3.0 meter intervals with a minimum embedment depth of 600 mm. Clips or tie wire should secure the coil to the fence structure at 300–500 mm intervals, with military-specification installations using 300 mm clip spacing and double-clip attachment at each post. All clip materials should be hot-dip galvanized to match the corrosion resistance of the razor wire itself.
