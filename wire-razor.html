Product Overview

Maximum Perimeter Protection With Razor Wire

Razor wire is a high-security blade-style fencing material engineered for applications where maximum deterrence is required. Sharp steel blades are securely attached to a high tensile steel wire core, creating a barrier that is exceptionally difficult to climb or cut through.

Available in galvanized, stainless steel (SS304/SS316), and PVC-coated finishes, our razor wire is manufactured to strict standards with consistent blade sharpness, uniform coil diameter, and reliable tensile strength — ideal for military installations, correctional facilities, border security, and critical infrastructure.

  • Cross concertina, coil, flat & welded mesh types
  • Coil diameters from 300mm to 980mm
  • Core wire 2.5–4.0mm, blade 0.4–0.6mm
  • Galvanized, stainless & PVC finishes
  • Certified tensile and shear strength
  • Tamper-resistant, difficult to breach

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about razor wire specification, coils, and installation.

What is the difference between razor wire and barbed wire?
Razor wire uses flat, stamped steel blades with a sharp, exposed edge attached to a high-tensile core, delivering a far higher deterrence and cutting risk than barbed wire's round twisted strands with pointed barbs. Razor wire is the standard for high-security perimeters, while barbed wire suits general, agricultural, and lower-risk fencing.
Should I choose galvanized or stainless steel razor wire?
Galvanized razor wire is economical and offers good corrosion resistance for most inland installations, lasting many years. Stainless steel (SS304/SS316) and heavy hot-dip galvanized/PVC finishes are best for coastal, humid, or chemically aggressive environments. If long service life in harsh conditions is the priority, choose stainless or PVC-coated razor wire.
How is razor wire measured and what coil diameter do I need?
Razor wire is specified by core wire diameter, blade material and width, and coil diameter. Coil diameters typically range from 300mm to 980mm. Larger diameters provide a taller, more intimidating barrier. Cross concertina is measured by its four intersecting blade points. Tell us your fence height and threat level to select the right coil.
Can I install razor wire myself?
Due to the sharp blades, razor wire installation requires professional handling, proper personal protective equipment (heavy gloves, protective clothing, safety glasses), and experience to avoid serious injury. Users should also comply with local regulations, which may restrict razor wire in residential and urban settings. We recommend professional installation by trained contractors.
Is razor wire legal and suitable for residential use?
Regulations vary by country and municipality. In many jurisdictions, razor wire is restricted or prohibited for residential use due to injury risk and liability, or requires special permits and height limits with signage. It is most commonly permitted for industrial, commercial, and government perimeter security. Check local laws before specifying razor wire for a home boundary.
How much razor wire do I need for my perimeter?
Estimate by measuring your perimeter length and the number of coil rows you plan. For a single top line of concertina, each meter of fence generally needs roughly one meter of deployed coil (with typical 300–450mm coil pitch), so a 100m perimeter needs about 100m of coil. Add 5–10% for joints and overlaps. Contact us with your fence plan for an accurate figure.
What is the difference between concertina and flat wrap razor wire?
Concertina razor wire is coiled into self-supporting circular spirals that expand to form a tall, dense barrier, ideal for the top or full face of fences and walls. Flat wrap razor wire is wound in a tight, low-profile helix along the fence top, offering a cleaner look while still deterring climbing. Choose concertina for maximum barrier volume, flat wrap for a compact straight topping.
How long does razor wire last?
With proper installation and coating selection, galvanized razor wire can last 10 to 20 years or more, while stainless steel razor wire can last 30+ years with virtually no maintenance. Service life depends on coating weight, climate, and exposure. Regular inspection and touch-up of damaged coating help maximize longevity and maintain deterrent effectiveness.
