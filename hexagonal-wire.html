Product Overview

Flexible, Lightweight & Cost-Effective

Hexagonal wire mesh, also commonly referred to as chicken wire, poultry netting, or hex mesh, is one of the most widely used wire mesh products in the world. Its distinctive hexagonal pattern is created by twisting pairs of wires together during the weaving process, forming a strong yet flexible mesh structure.

The product has been in use for over 150 years, originally developed for poultry enclosures but now serving countless applications across agriculture, construction, landscaping, and industrial sectors. The hexagonal opening provides an optimal balance of visibility, airflow, and strength.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about hexagonal wire mesh.

What is hexagonal wire mesh (chicken wire)?
Hexagonal wire mesh, commonly known as chicken wire or poultry netting, is a type of woven wire fencing made from galvanized or PVC coated steel wire. The wire is twisted together in a hexagonal pattern, creating a flexible, lightweight mesh with uniform hexagonal openings. It is one of the most versatile and cost-effective wire mesh products available, widely used for poultry enclosures, garden fencing, crop protection, and construction applications.
What is the difference between galvanized and PVC coated hex mesh?
Galvanized hexagonal wire mesh is coated with a layer of zinc through either electro-galvanizing or hot-dip galvanizing, which provides good corrosion resistance for most outdoor applications. Hot-dip galvanized mesh offers thicker zinc coating and longer service life. PVC coated hex mesh has an additional layer of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) over the galvanized wire, providing superior corrosion resistance, UV protection, and a variety of color options. PVC coated mesh typically lasts 2-3 times longer than standard galvanized mesh, especially in harsh or coastal environments.
What mesh size should I choose for poultry fencing?
For poultry fencing, a mesh size of 1 inch (25.4mm) or 2 inch (50.8mm) is most commonly recommended. The 1 inch mesh provides better protection against small predators like snakes, weasels, and small rodents, while 2 inch mesh is suitable for larger poultry like chickens and turkeys where predator risk is lower. For chicks and small birds, a smaller mesh size of 1/2 inch (12.7mm) is recommended to prevent escape and protect from small predators.
How long does hexagonal wire mesh last?
The lifespan of hexagonal wire mesh depends on the coating type, wire gauge, and environmental conditions. Electro-galvanized mesh typically lasts 3-7 years, while hot-dip galvanized mesh can last 10-20 years. PVC coated hex mesh offers the longest service life, typically 15-30+ years depending on the thickness of the PVC coating. In coastal or highly corrosive environments, PVC coated mesh is strongly recommended for maximum longevity.
Can hexagonal wire mesh be used for plastering?
Yes, hexagonal wire mesh (often referred to as plastering mesh or chicken wire) is widely used as a lath for plastering and stucco applications. When attached to wall framing or masonry surfaces, it provides a mechanical key for the plaster or stucco to adhere to, preventing cracking and ensuring a durable finish. For plastering applications, a mesh size of 1 inch or 3/4 inch with a heavier wire gauge (BWG 20-22) is typically recommended. Galvanized mesh is preferred for plastering to prevent rust staining.
How do I install hexagonal wire mesh fencing?
Installing hexagonal wire mesh fencing is straightforward: 1) Clear the fence line and set wooden or metal posts at 6-10 foot intervals, 2) Unroll the mesh along the fence line starting from one end, 3) Attach the mesh to the posts using fencing staples (for wood posts) or wire ties (for metal posts), spacing fasteners every 12-18 inches, 4) Overlap mesh sections by at least one hexagonal opening and wire them together, 5) Bury the bottom edge 6-12 inches into the ground to prevent animals from digging under, 6) Cut the mesh using wire cutters or tin snips. The flexible nature of hex mesh makes it easy to contour to uneven terrain.
