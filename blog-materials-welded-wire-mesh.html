Materials for Welded Wire Mesh

Introduction

kestrel metal manufactures all products in a bare, mill-finished condition. This process supports our goal to produce superior welded wire mesh efficiently, bringing you the best value possible. Every production run records and uses materials that are inspected and certified before manufacturing. Wire mill certificates of compliance are always available upon request, free of charge.

Stainless Steel Wire Mesh

Stainless steel is commonly used in construction and industrial fields. Various types of stainless steel can be made into wire mesh to improve corrosion resistance, formability, and aesthetics. Stainless steel is made by adding chromium to steel. Chromium forms an oxide layer on the surface called the "passivation layer." This layer protects the surface and prevents further corrosion. Many types of stainless steel also add nickel, molybdenum, titanium, manganese, and other metals.

Note: When specifying stainless steel, please contact kestrelmetal for material considerations.

Common Types

Architectural wire mesh: EPQ quality T304 stainless steel and EPQ quality T316 stainless steel wire.

Industrial wire mesh: T304, T309, T310, T316, T317, T321, T347, T410, T430, and 2205 duplex stainless steel.

Carbon Steel Wire Mesh (普通钢丝网)

Carbon steel—also known as "low carbon steel"—typically contains 6% to 12% carbon and is made by cold drawing hot-rolled steel bars. This material offers excellent value and is widely used for its good weldability, strength, and economy. kestrel metal produces its own carbon steel wire for woven and welded wire mesh products.

Considerations

  • Bare carbon steel will rust under certain conditions.
  • Electroplating or powder coating are surface treatment options for indoor applications.
  • For outdoor powder-coated applications, consider pre-galvanized wire mesh as an alternative.

Weathering Steel (耐候钢)

Compared to ordinary steel, weathering steel has a chemical composition that gives it greater resistance to atmospheric corrosion. Weathering steel gradually rusts when exposed to wind and sun, forming a protective film that prevents internal corrosion. This material can be used for many purposes without painting. Over time, weathering steel changes from yellow-orange to deep rust orange, and finally to dark brown.

Considerations

  • Weathering steel is most commonly used in outdoor applications.
  • Note that rain runoff may stain adjacent materials like stone or concrete.
  • In humid and subtropical climates, the rust layer may not stabilize.

Pre-Galvanized Wire Mesh (预镀锌钢丝网)

kestrel metal offers two types of pre-galvanized wire mesh: "regular coating" and the registered trademark "Galfan." Pre-galvanized means the wire is coated with zinc before being made into wire mesh. This differs from "post-galvanized" where the finished mesh is dipped in molten zinc.

Regular galvanized wire: A thin layer of zinc is applied to carbon steel during the drawing process. Slightly higher price than carbon steel but better corrosion resistance. Ideal base material for outdoor powder-coated wire mesh.

Considerations For outdoor powder-coated applications, use pre-galvanized wire to provide a zinc layer under wire intersection points.

Galfan Wire Mesh

Galfan is carbon steel wire with a coating of 95% zinc and 5% aluminum. The thicker zinc coating makes this wire more corrosion-resistant than "regular coating" pre-galvanized wire. The aluminum in the mixture maintains high ductility, preventing coating loss during wire mesh manufacturing.

Considerations

  • Galfan coating thickness is similar to hot-dip galvanizing.
  • Galfan has "self-healing" ability for minor scratches.
  • If heavy processing is required, hot-dip galvanizing after processing is recommended.

Aluminum Wire Mesh

Aluminum weighs only one-third of steel, making it highly adaptable for woven mesh products. Aluminum wire mesh has excellent atmospheric corrosion resistance, reasonable price, and various surface treatment options. Ideal when weight and corrosion resistance are primary considerations.

Considerations Aluminum has a relatively low elastic modulus. Aluminum mesh deforms more under the same load compared to steel mesh. Consider increasing wire diameter for required stiffness.

Common alloys: A1350 (standard), A5056, A6061

Bronze Wire Mesh

Bronze is a metal alloy primarily composed of copper with tin added. The most common bronze alloy in wire mesh manufacturing is C220 "commercial bronze." Its unique bright caramel color naturally darkens over time and can be chemically colored.

Architectural Applications

  • Bronze wire is drawn to a bright caramel color.
  • Bronze woven with stainless steel creates subtle contrast.
  • Bronze is considered a "living finish" with rapid natural color changes.

Considerations When kestrel metal weaves with bronze wire, it is initially "bright" but considered "bare." The bronze will almost immediately discolor due to oxidation. To maintain a "bright" effect, the oxide layer must be removed before applying a clear coating.

Brass Wire Mesh

Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc. By changing the ratio, various brass alloys can be obtained. Brass has explosion-proof properties, making it ideal for flammable sensitive areas. Brass has a bright golden appearance and is relatively resistant to oxidation.

Architectural Applications

  • Brass wire is drawn to a bright golden luster.
  • Brass woven with stainless steel creates subtle contrast.
  • Brass transitions from bright to green, eventually forming a brown patina.

Considerations When woven, brass wire is initially "bright" but considered "bare." To maintain the final "bright" effect, the oxide layer must be removed before applying a clear coating.

Common alloys: C230 (red brass), C260 (cartridge brass), C270 (yellow brass)

Copper Wire Mesh

Copper wire has excellent electrical and thermal conductivity. Copper has high corrosion resistance in marine environments, making it suitable for saline environments. Copper also resists marine organisms like snails and mussels and has antibacterial properties.

Architectural Applications

  • Pure C110 copper wire is drawn to a bright salmon-red color.
  • Copper provides rich color contrast when woven with stainless steel.
  • Copper transitions from bright to brown, gray, and finally blue-green or gray-green patina.

Considerations When woven, copper wire is initially "bright" but considered "bare." To maintain the final "bright" effect, the oxide layer must be removed before applying a clear coating.
