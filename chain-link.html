Overview

What Is Chain Link Fence?

Chain link fencing is one of the most widely used fencing systems in the world. Constructed from interwoven galvanized or PVC coated steel wires, it creates a distinctive diamond pattern that provides excellent visibility, airflow, and security.

Suitable for residential yards, commercial perimeters, sports facilities, schools, construction sites, and agricultural properties. Available in multiple mesh sizes, wire gauges, heights, and coating options to meet any application requirement.

  • Galvanized steel or PVC coated wire
  • Mesh sizes: 50×50mm, 60×60mm, 75×75mm
  • Heights from 3ft to 12ft (0.9m–3.6m)
  • Zinc coating 40–270 g/m² for corrosion resistance
  • PVC colors: green, black, white, brown + custom

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard height for a chain link fence?
The most common heights for chain link fences are 3ft, 4ft, 5ft, 6ft, 8ft, 10ft, and 12ft. For residential properties, 4ft to 6ft is typical. Commercial and industrial applications often use 8ft to 12ft heights for enhanced security. Sports facilities may use 10ft or higher for baseball backstops and tennis court enclosures.
How long does a chain link fence last?
A properly installed chain link fence can last 15 to 50 years depending on the material and coating. Galvanized chain link fences typically last 15–30 years, while PVC-coated fences can last 25–50+ years due to the additional corrosion protection. Stainless steel chain link offers the longest service life, especially in harsh or coastal environments.
What is the difference between galvanized and PVC coated chain link?
Galvanized chain link is coated with a layer of zinc through hot-dip or electro-galvanization, providing excellent rust resistance with a silver metallic appearance. PVC coated chain link has an additional layer of polyvinyl chloride over the galvanized wire, offering superior corrosion protection and available in various colors (green, black, white, brown). PVC coated fencing is ideal for residential applications where aesthetics are important.
How much does chain link fencing cost?
Chain link fencing is one of the most affordable fencing options. Costs vary based on height, gauge, coating type, and installation complexity. As a general guide, galvanized chain link typically costs less than PVC-coated versions. Factors such as terrain, gate requirements, and local labor rates also affect the total cost. Contact us for a detailed quote tailored to your specific project requirements.
Can chain link fences be used for residential properties?
Yes, chain link fences are very popular for residential applications. They are commonly used for yard enclosures, pool safety fences, garden protection, and pet containment. PVC-coated chain link in green or black is particularly popular for residential use as it blends well with landscaping. Privacy slats can also be added to chain link fences for increased privacy without sacrificing durability.
What gauge wire is best for chain link fencing?
The best wire gauge depends on the application. For residential fencing, 11–12 gauge (2.7mm–3.0mm) is commonly used and provides a good balance of strength and cost. For commercial and industrial applications, 9–10 gauge (3.0mm–3.5mm) offers greater strength and durability. Heavier 6–8 gauge wire is used for high-security applications. A lower gauge number indicates a thicker, stronger wire.
How do I install a chain link fence?
Chain link fence installation involves several key steps: 1) Plan and mark the fence line, 2) Dig post holes at regular intervals (typically 6–10 feet apart), 3) Set terminal posts (corner, end, and gate posts) in concrete, 4) Set line posts in concrete, 5) Attach tension bands, top rails, and brace bands to terminal posts, 6) Unroll and stretch the chain link mesh fabric, 7) Attach the mesh to the framework using fence ties, 8) Install gates and hardware. Professional installation is recommended for best results.
What fittings do I need for a chain link fence?
A complete chain link fence installation requires several fittings: terminal posts (end and corner posts), line posts (intermediate support posts), top rails, top rail fittings (caps and sleeves), tension bands (to attach mesh to terminal posts), tension bars, brace bands, fence ties (aluminum or galvanized wire ties to attach mesh to line posts), post caps, gate frames, gate hinges, and gate latches. The exact quantity depends on the fence height, length, and configuration.
