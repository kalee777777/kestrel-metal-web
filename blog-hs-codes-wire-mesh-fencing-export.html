HS Codes for Wire Mesh, Fencing & Razor Wire: Complete Export Classification Guide (2026)

August 21, 2026 9 min read
Back to Blog & News

HS (Harmonized System) codes for wire mesh and fencing products exported from China fall primarily under Chapter 73 (Articles of Iron or Steel), with key classifications including 7308.90 (structures and parts of structures), 7314.41 (galvanized woven wire mesh), 7314.42 (PVC coated woven wire mesh), 7314.49 (other woven wire), 7313.00 (barbed wire), and 7217.10 (galvanized wire). Correct HS code classification is critical for determining import duty rates, trade agreement eligibility, and customs clearance speed in destination markets including the EU, UK, US, Australia, and Canada. Understanding HS Chapter 73: Articles of Iron or Steel

Chapter 73 of the Harmonized System covers all articles of iron or steel, and is the primary chapter for the vast majority of wire mesh and fencing products manufactured and exported from China. The chapter is subdivided by manufacturing process and product type: heading 7217 covers iron or steel wire (both galvanized and plain, the raw material for mesh production), heading 7313 covers barbed wire of all types, heading 7314 covers wire mesh (woven, knitted, or welded), and heading 7308 covers structures and parts of structures — which includes prefabricated fence panels, gate assemblies, fence post systems, and complete perimeter fence kits when shipped in assembled or kit form rather than as raw mesh rolls.

Understanding which heading applies is determined by the form and state of the product at the time of export. Raw chain link mesh rolled onto a spool, without posts, clamps, or fittings, classifies under 7314.41 (galvanized) or 7314.42 (PVC coated). A complete chain link fence kit that includes mesh rolls, line posts, terminal posts, tension bars, and gate hardware, sold as a single commercial unit, may classify under 7308.90 instead. The distinction matters because 7308.90 carries a different tariff schedule in certain markets and may qualify for different duty preferences depending on the importing country. Kestrel Metal's commercial invoices always state the HS code used alongside a detailed goods description to ensure the customs broker and importing buyer have the information needed for an accurate classification at the port of entry.

Why HS Code Classification Matters for Fencing Export

Correct HS code classification is not merely a bureaucratic formality — it directly determines the import duty rate applied at the destination port, the eligibility for preferential tariff treatment under free trade agreements, and in some cases the anti-dumping or countervailing duties that may apply. A misclassified shipment can result in overpayment of duties (if classified under a higher-rated heading), rejection at customs (if the code contradicts the product description or certificate of origin), or penalties and retroactive assessments if the error is discovered during a post-clearance audit. For wire mesh and fencing exporters in Anping, Hebei — the world's largest wire mesh manufacturing cluster — getting the HS code right on every commercial invoice is essential for maintaining buyer confidence and avoiding costly delays in destination markets.

The duty rate implications are significant across all major import markets. In the European Union, most Chapter 73 wire mesh and fencing products carry a 0% MFN duty rate, which means the HS code does not materially affect the duty amount for standard MFN-rated shipments. However, the HS code determines eligibility for preferential treatment under the EU–China bilateral framework and, more importantly, under the RCEP agreement, which came into force for China in January 2022 and provides reduced or zero-duty access for qualifying products when a valid Certificate of Origin is presented. In the United States, duty rates for wire mesh under 7314.41 and 7314.42 range from 0% to 3.4% depending on the specific subheading and product specifications, making correct classification directly relevant to landed cost calculations. For Australia, which is a founding RCEP member, wire mesh products under Chapter 73 generally enter at 0% duty, but the HS code must match the FTA preference criteria for the Certificate of Origin to be accepted by Australian Border Force.

Product-by-Product Classification Guide

Chain Link Fence — HS 7314.41 or 7314.42

Chain link fence (also known as cyclone fence or diamond mesh) is classified under heading 7314 as woven wire mesh. The specific subheading depends on the surface treatment: 7314.41 applies to galvanized (zinc-coated) chain link fabric that has been coated after weaving, while 7314.42 applies to chain link fabric that has been further coated with plastics (PVC or polyester) after galvanizing. Both subheadings cover chain link rolls where the product is shipped as raw mesh without posts or hardware. Kestrel Metal supplies chain link fence in standard rolls from 0.9 m to 3.7 m height, mesh openings from 25 mm to 50 mm, and wire gauges from 9 BWG to 12.5 BWG — all classified under 7314.41 (galvanized) or 7314.42 (PVC coated).

3D Wire Panel Fence — HS 7308.90

3D wire panel fence (also called V-mesh fence or 3D security fence) is typically classified under 7308.90 when shipped as complete fence panels with integrated V-ribs or bending profiles, rather than as raw woven mesh. The reason is that 3D wire panels are manufactured to specific dimensions and profiles designed for direct installation as perimeter fence structures, which aligns with the heading 7308 description of “structures and parts of structures of iron or steel.” Posts, clamps, and fittings shipped alongside the panels in the same commercial consignment reinforce the 7308.90 classification. Kestrel Metal supplies 3D wire panels in heights from 1.0 m to 2.5 m with mesh openings of 50 × 200 mm and wire diameters of 4.0 mm / 5.0 mm, all classified under 7308.90 for export to EU, UK, and Middle Eastern markets.

Gabion Boxes — HS 7308.90

Gabion boxes (wire mesh baskets filled with stone for retaining walls, erosion control, and landscape architecture) are classified under 7308.90 when shipped as assembled or foldable basket units. The woven or welded wire mesh that forms the gabion structure itself may individually classify under 7314.49 (other woven wire products), but once the mesh is fabricated into gabion basket form — with lid panels, lacing wire, and connection hardware — the commercial product is treated as a structure or part of a structure. Kestrel Metal supplies woven and welded gabion boxes in standard sizes from 1 × 1 × 1 m to 2 × 1 × 1 m, with mesh openings of 80 × 100 mm and wire diameters of 2.7 mm to 3.2 mm, hot-dip galvanized or Galfan (Zn–5Al alloy) coated to EN 10244-2.

Razor Wire — HS 7217.10 or 7313.00

Razor wire (also known as barbed tape or concertina wire) presents a dual classification depending on the component and form. The galvanized steel line wire and razor tape strip components classify under 7217.10 (galvanized iron/steel wire) when exported as raw material. Finished razor wire products — including concertina coils (BTC), flat wrap coils, single coil systems, and razor wire welded to mesh — may alternatively classify under 7313.00 (barbed wire and barbed wire types) in some markets, or under 7308.90 when shipped as part of a complete security fence system. Kestrel Metal recommends that importers confirm the preferred classification with their customs broker, as some national tariff schedules apply different anti-dumping measures to 7217.10 vs. 7313.00. Our technical team provides dual HS code documentation on all razor wire invoices to support both classification positions.

Barbed Wire — HS 7313.00

Traditional barbed wire (single twist, double twist, and PVC coated varieties) classifies unambiguously under 7313.00 — “Barbed wire and other twisted iron or steel wire, of iron or steel.” This heading covers all forms of barbed wire regardless of whether the base wire is galvanized or PVC coated, and regardless of the barb spacing, barb length, or twist configuration. The duty rate for 7313.00 is 0% in the EU, US, and Australia, making this one of the most straightforward classifications in the fencing category. Kestrel Metal supplies barbed wire in standard coils of 25 kg, 50 kg, and 100 kg with wire gauges from 1.6 mm to 2.8 mm and barb spacings of 75 mm, 100 mm, and 125 mm.

Stainless Steel Wire Mesh — HS 7229.90

Stainless steel wire mesh (welded or woven) classifies under 7229.90 (stainless steel wire, other) when the mesh is in roll or sheet form without further fabrication into structures. Stainless steel welded wire mesh panels for security fencing, industrial screening, or architectural applications may alternatively classify under 7308.90 if shipped as prefabricated structural panels with mounting hardware. The 0% MFN duty rate applies across all major markets for both 7229.90 and 7308.90, so the classification choice is driven primarily by the form of the product and the certificate of origin requirements rather than by duty rate optimization. Kestrel Metal supplies SS welded wire mesh in grades 304 and 316, with mesh openings from 12.5 mm to 50 mm and wire diameters from 1.5 mm to 5.0 mm.

Common Classification Mistakes to Avoid

The following are the most frequent HS code classification errors encountered in wire mesh and fencing exports from China. Each mistake can result in customs delays, retroactive duty assessments, or rejection of FTA preferential tariff claims.

  • Using 7314.41 for assembled fence kits. If the shipment includes mesh, posts, gates, and fittings as a complete system, the correct classification is typically 7308.90 (structures and parts), not 7314.41 (wire mesh only). Classifying a complete fence kit under 7314.41 will raise red flags at customs because the product description will not match the heading scope.
  • Confusing 7313.00 with 7314.41 for razor wire. Barbed wire is 7313.00; razor wire (concertina coils) may be 7217.10, 7313.00, or 7308.90 depending on form. Do not use 7314.41 for razor wire under any circumstances — this heading covers woven mesh, not barbed or razor-type products.
  • Forgetting to update the HS code when product form changes. If you normally export chain link mesh in rolls (7314.41) but a new order ships the same mesh as part of a fence kit with posts and fittings, the HS code must change to 7308.90. Using the old code from a previous commercial invoice creates a documentation mismatch that customs auditors will flag.
  • Using 6-digit HS codes without the national tariff line extension. The Harmonized System provides 6-digit codes at the international level, but most importing countries require 8-, 10-, or 12-digit tariff line numbers for customs declarations. Always confirm the full national tariff line code with your customs broker before the ship sails.
  • Misclassifying gabion mesh rolls as 7308.90. Gabion mesh supplied as raw rolls (without basket fabrication) classifies under 7314.49, not 7308.90. Only fabricated gabion baskets with lids and connection wire qualify for 7308.90.

Documentation Requirements for HS Code Compliance

Proper HS code classification must be supported by accurate commercial documentation. Kestrel Metal provides the following documents with every export shipment to ensure smooth customs clearance at the destination port:

  • Commercial Invoice — includes the HS code (6-digit international + national extension where known), full product description, unit price, total value, Incoterms, country of origin, and consignee details. The commercial invoice is the primary document customs authorities use to verify HS code classification.
  • Packing List — details the quantity, weight, dimensions, and packaging type for each line item in the shipment. Packing list data must be consistent with the commercial invoice and the Bill of Lading.
  • Certificate of Origin — issued by the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT) or by a recognized chamber of commerce. Required for FTA preferential tariff claims under RCEP, ASEAN–China FTA, China–Australia FTA, or other bilateral agreements. The HS code on the Certificate of Origin must match the commercial invoice.
  • Mill Test Certificate / Test Report — confirms the material composition, wire diameter, zinc coating mass, and mechanical properties of the product. The test report should reference the applicable EN or ASTM standard and cross-reference the production batch number.
  • Bill of Lading — the shipping document issued by the carrier confirming the goods have been loaded for transport. The Bill of Lading description should align with the commercial invoice product description and HS code.

RCEP & Trade Agreement Benefits

The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) agreement, which came into force for China on January 1, 2022, provides preferential (reduced or zero) duty rates for qualifying wire mesh and fencing products exported to member states including Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, and the ten ASEAN nations. To claim RCEP preferential tariffs, the exporter must issue a Certificate of Origin (CO) that confirms the product satisfies the RCEP Rules of Origin — typically requiring that the product is manufactured entirely within a member state using raw materials sourced from member states, or that it meets a tariff shift or regional value content threshold.

For wire mesh and fencing products manufactured at Kestrel Metal's factory in Anping, Hebei, the RCEP origin criteria are straightforward because the entire production chain — from steel wire drawing to galvanizing to mesh weaving or welding to final quality inspection — takes place within a single RCEP member state (China). This ensures that the finished product qualifies as originating in China under RCEP, and a valid CCPIT-issued Certificate of Origin will enable the importer to claim the RCEP preferential duty rate at the port of entry. For Australian importers, RCEP eliminates duty on most Chapter 73 wire mesh products that previously carried a 0% rate under the China–Australia FTA (ChAFTA) — but RCEP provides an additional layer of documentation security and is the preferred origin framework for new shipments from 2023 onward.

Beyond RCEP, China maintains several bilateral free trade agreements that may provide preferential treatment for wire mesh exports. The ASEAN–China FTA (ACFTA) provides zero-duty access for most Chapter 73 products to Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos, and Brunei. The China–Pakistan FTA, China–Chile FTA, China–Peru FTA, and China–New Zealand FTA each carry their own Rules of Origin requirements and preferential tariff schedules. For EU and UK markets, China does not currently have a bilateral FTA providing tariff preferences for steel products, so MFN rates apply. For the United States, China–US trade is subject to Section 301 tariffs in addition to standard MFN duties, making the HS code classification critical for determining the total landed cost. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What HS code should I use for galvanized chain link fence shipped without posts?

A: Galvanized chain link fence fabric shipped as a roll without posts, clamps, or other fittings classifies under HS 7314.41 (woven wire mesh, galvanized, coated after weaving). If the chain link is further coated with PVC or polyester, the correct code is 7314.42. Only when the chain link is shipped as part of a complete fence system with posts and hardware should 7308.90 be used instead.

Q: Do I need a Certificate of Origin to import wire mesh into the EU?

A: A Certificate of Origin is not mandatory for EU imports of wire mesh because most Chapter 73 products enter the EU at a 0% MFN duty rate. However, if you wish to claim preferential treatment under any applicable FTA framework or need the CO for your internal compliance records, Kestrel Metal can issue a CCPIT Certificate of Origin with every shipment. The CO is required for RCEP preferential claims in Australia, Japan, South Korea, and ASEAN markets.

Q: What is the difference between HS code 7314.41 and 7308.90 for fence products?

A: HS 7314.41 covers galvanized woven wire mesh in roll or sheet form — raw mesh without further fabrication into structures. HS 7308.90 covers structures and parts of structures, including prefabricated fence panels, complete fence kits with posts and fittings, and gabion baskets. The determining factor is the form and completeness of the product at the time of export: mesh rolls = 7314.41; assembled fence systems = 7308.90.
