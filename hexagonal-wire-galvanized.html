Product Information

Galvanized hexagonal wire netting, also known as chicken wire or poultry netting, is manufactured from low carbon steel wire with a zinc coating applied through either hot-dip galvanizing or electro galvanizing. The galvanized coating provides excellent protection against rust and corrosion, making it ideal for outdoor applications.

Available in both normal twist and reverse twist weaving patterns. The reverse twist design offers greater structural stability as adjacent wires are twisted in opposite directions, preventing the mesh from unraveling if a single wire is cut. Normal twist is more economical and suitable for general-purpose applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about galvanized hexagonal wire netting.

What is galvanized hexagonal wire mesh?
Galvanized hexagonal wire mesh, commonly known as chicken wire or poultry netting, is a woven wire fencing product made by twisting low-carbon steel wires into uniform hexagonal openings and protecting them with a zinc coating. It is lightweight, flexible and widely used for poultry, garden, farm and construction applications.
What is the difference between electro and hot-dip galvanizing?
Electro galvanizing applies a thinner, more uniform zinc layer for economical general-purpose use. Hot-dip galvanizing immerses the finished wire in molten zinc, creating a thicker coating and longer service life for demanding outdoor or humid environments.
What mesh size should I choose for poultry fencing?
A 1-inch or 2-inch mesh is commonly selected for chickens and larger poultry. For chicks, small birds or areas with small predators, choose a smaller 1/2-inch or 3/8-inch opening. The final choice should consider bird size, predator risk and the required wire gauge.
What is the difference between normal twist and reverse twist?
Normal twist is an economical pattern for general-purpose fencing. Reverse twist alternates the twisting direction of adjacent wires, improving structural stability and helping prevent the mesh from unraveling if one wire is cut.
Can galvanized hexagonal wire mesh be used for plastering?
Yes. Hexagonal mesh is often used as plastering or stucco lath because it provides a mechanical key for the coating to grip. A 3/4-inch or 1-inch mesh with a heavier wire gauge is commonly selected, and galvanized wire helps reduce rust staining.
How do I install galvanized hexagonal wire mesh?
Set timber or metal posts along the fence line, unroll the mesh, fasten it with staples or wire ties, overlap adjacent sections by at least one opening, and secure the bottom edge against digging. Wire cutters or tin snips can be used to trim the roll on site.
